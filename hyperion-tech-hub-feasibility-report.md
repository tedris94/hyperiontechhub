1. Executive Summary
Hyperion Tech Hub intends to establish a physical office and training center that provides technology services, education, and enterprise software solutions. The hub will serve individuals, schools, and businesses in Abuja and surrounding areas with a mix of walk-in services, structured training programs, and contracted software delivery.

This feasibility study evaluates market demand, operational capability, technical requirements, legal considerations, and financial viability. The analysis supports the launch of the hub with a phased implementation plan, diversified revenue streams, and manageable risk exposure.

2. Project Description and Objectives
Project: Establish a physical Hyperion Tech Hub facility to deliver technology services, training, and systems development.

Objectives:
- Offer a one-stop destination for tech training, repairs, printing, and software solutions.
- Build recurring revenue from schools and corporate customers.
- Develop in-house capacity for web, mobile, and enterprise systems.
- Create a scalable operational model for future expansion.

3. Services Scope
Core services in scope:
- AI and Native Coding
- WordPress and Web Development
- Cloud Services
- Android Development
- Corporate Training
- Online Courses
- Computer and Laptop Repairs
- Graphic Design and Printing
- Cybersecurity
- Computer Studies Center
- School Portal System
- Student Management System
- Result System
- Custom App Development (Web, Desktop, Mobile)

4. Market Feasibility
Demand drivers:
- Growing need for digital literacy and job-ready skills.
- Increased demand for web and mobile solutions in local businesses.
- Schools moving to digital portals and results systems.
- Consistent foot traffic for printing, scanning, and repairs.
- Rising need for cybersecurity and cloud migration support.

Target customers:
- Schools and educational institutions.
- SMEs and startups needing digital systems.
- Students and job seekers seeking tech skills.
- General public for printing and repairs.

Competitive advantage:
- Multi-service offering under one roof.
- Combination of training and delivery.
- Professional branding and operational structure.

5. Technical Feasibility
Required infrastructure:
- 15 to 30 desktop workstations.
- High-speed internet with backup connection.
- Laser and color printers, photocopier, scanner.
- Backup power (generator/inverter/solar).
- Secure network and access controls.

Technical capability:
- Web, mobile, and desktop development.
- Learning management and curriculum delivery.
- Onsite repair and diagnostics.
- Cloud tooling, backup, and security monitoring.

6. Operational Feasibility
Proposed staffing (phase 1):
- Hub Manager
- 2 to 3 Trainers
- Front Desk and Print Officer
- Software Developer
- Hardware Technician
- IT Support and Network Admin

Operating model:
- Walk-in services for printing and repairs.
- Scheduled classes for training programs.
- Contract delivery for software systems.
- Maintenance and support plans for business clients.

7. Legal and Regulatory Feasibility
Key compliance areas:
- Business registration and tax compliance.
- Software licensing and copyright compliance.
- Data protection for student records and client data.
- Service agreements and terms for software clients.

8. Financial Feasibility
The project is viable with a balanced mix of training revenue, services, and software delivery. The model assumes:
- A ramp-up period of 3 months.
- Diverse monthly revenue streams.
- Stable operating costs with moderate growth.

Summary:
- Estimated startup cost: NGN 12,000,000
- Target monthly revenue at maturity: NGN 4,350,000
- Estimated monthly net profit at maturity: NGN 998,000
- Break-even: Months 15 to 17

9. Risk Assessment and Mitigation
Operational risk: Power instability
Mitigation: Backup power and surge protection.

Market risk: Slow enrollment in training programs
Mitigation: Partnerships with schools, flexible pricing, and short-term cohorts.

Execution risk: Project delays in software delivery
Mitigation: Agile planning, clear scope, and milestone payments.

Financial risk: Underestimated costs
Mitigation: Contingency budget and phased growth.

10. Implementation Plan
Phase 1 (Months 1 to 2)
- Secure location, renovate, install equipment.
- Launch printing and basic training.

Phase 2 (Months 3 to 6)
- Launch software delivery services.
- Begin school portal pilots.

Phase 3 (Months 6 to 12)
- Expand class size, corporate training.
- Scale system maintenance contracts.

11. Conclusion and Recommendation
The proposed Hyperion Tech Hub is feasible with strong market demand, diversified revenue, and manageable operational requirements. A phased launch with consistent marketing and school partnerships is recommended.
